Why did my seed produce unexpected results?

A seed decides what the run generates. It does not decide what you walk into, or who is standing next to you. Almost every report of a wrong seed turns out to be one of these, so it is worth a read before you go looking for a bug.

Set the lobby up the same way

Join order. P1 is whoever joins first. Neow, the Ancients, card rewards and shop relics are all rolled per slot, so two players joining in the other order swaps everything that belongs to them. This is the single most common cause of a seed looking wrong.

Player count. Two, three and four players are different runs from the same seed. The count decides which Ancient opens each act, and a chest puts one relic on the table per player.

Characters. A player’s card pool, shop relics and own relic bag all belong to their character, so a last-minute switch changes what that player is offered.

Ascension. Ascension 10 gives the final act a second boss. From Ascension 7 the card rarity odds tighten, which moves every card reward after the first fight. Set the level you are actually going to play and both take care of themselves.

What everybody has unlocked. A relic pool is filtered by what that player has revealed, and the run uses everyone’s unlocks combined. Only your own save is readable from this machine, so if a partner has revealed more or less than you, their offers and parts of the run can differ from what you see here.

The game version. Predictions are computed for one build. A patch resizes the content pools, pool size decides how many draws a shuffle costs, and everything after that lands somewhere else. A banner appears above the results when your install and this tool disagree. Mods that add content do the same thing, so relic, card, event or encounter mods are called out the same way. Purely cosmetic ones are fine: art replacements and reskins change nothing a seed decides. The banner cannot tell which kind you have, so it warns whenever a mods folder exists at all.

Once you are playing

Card rewards past the first fight need those fights back to back. Fight 1 is safe: the first room after the Ancient is always a monster room, whichever way you go. Fights 2 and 3 assume you walk straight into the next monster room each time, with no shop, elite, event or rest in between. Take a detour and the prediction is for a fight you never had, and the further along it is the easier that is to do by accident.

Neow options that hand out cards. Arcane Scroll, Hefty Tablet, Massive Scroll, Scroll Boxes and Neow’s Bones draw from the same stream the fight rewards use, so taking one shifts both of that player’s fights. It affects only the player who took it. Every other Neow choice is free.

Somebody emptied the chest early. A chest pulls from one bag the whole party shares, and anything anyone picks up is taken out of it: elite rewards, relic events, and a merchant’s stock, which is removed when the shop is stocked rather than when something is bought. The rarities stay exact. The relics slide along, which is what the “allow taken earlier” setting is for.

A ? room turned into a treasure room. It happens at a small but growing chance each act, and it runs a full round of chest picks, moving every later chest along. Tell the search about it and the rest lines back up.

Shops are counted by visits, not floors. The third relic slot follows a fixed order, so walking past a merchant moves every later shop up a place.

Events are an order, not a schedule. Each act shuffles its events once and hands them out from the front, but a room gives you the next one you currently qualify for and have not already seen. Health, gold, deck contents and the act you are in all gate entries, so read these as “near the front of the queue”.

A profile that has not met every boss. If a boss exists that nobody in the lobby has seen yet, the game quietly forces it into the run in place of the one the seed rolled. That is not modelled here, so bosses on a fresh account can differ. It costs no randomness, so nothing else about the run moves with it.

Nothing on this list is a mistake in the seed. In every case the seed generated exactly what it says, and the run asked a different question of it.
